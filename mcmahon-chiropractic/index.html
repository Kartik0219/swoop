Chiropractic

The art and science of achieving the full potential of the individual, through the removal of any interference to the nervous system.

What Dr Cook looks at

  • The spine and limbs, for joints that have locked up
  • Physical knocks: car accidents, sport, gardening, work
  • Nutrition, such as a lack of magnesium or vitamins
  • Lifestyle: exercise and posture
  • Stress at work or at home

Your first consultation

  • An extensive case history
  • A physical examination
  • The results, discussed with you

Treatment may include

  • Muscle work: massage, ultrasound and stretching
  • Chiropractic adjustments, by hand or mechanically assisted
  • Advice for home, and sometimes changes to diet or supplements
  • X-rays from an x-ray clinic where clinically indicated, the cost usually covered by Medicare
  • Treatment changed to suit you, including with osteoporosis or after spinal surgery

Homoeopathy

Natural remedies that take the whole person into account: physical, emotional and mental health.

Where it comes from

  • A complete system of medicine
  • Developed by the German physician Dr Samuel Hahnemann
  • Based on the principle of “like cures like”
  • Used for first aid, and for acute or chronic complaints

Your first consultation

  • The problem that brought you in
  • Any other conditions you have
  • Your character and emotional state
  • A homoeopathic medicine, prescribed on the whole symptom picture

With your other medicines

  • Homoeopathic and pharmaceutical medicines can be used together
  • They work very differently and will not react with each other
  • Only stop a pharmaceutical medicine under the direction of your doctor

Orthotics

Your feet are the foundation for your whole body, and many people with foot problems feel little or no pain in their feet. Dr Cook often suggests Stabilizers: orthotics made by Foot Levelers, an American company that makes them for chiropractors.

Full size or ¾ length, depending on the kind of shoe you wear, and in different thicknesses for how active you are.

Mineral hair analysis

HTMA is a safe, non-invasive pathology test. It measures the levels and ratios of nutrient and toxic minerals found in hair.

  • Data on more than 35 nutrient and toxic minerals
  • Over 25 important mineral ratios
  • Health information often not shown in standard blood and urine tests
  • Personalised patient and doctor reports, with suggested changes to diet and supplements

Am I covered?

Pick the treatment and your cover to read the clinic’s own answer.

Treatment
Cover

Yes, with the right extras

All private health insurers will cover chiropractic treatment if you are on the right level of extras cover.

Not usually

Medicare doesn’t pay for chiropractic on its own. If you have a complex medical condition, your GP can refer you under the Medicare EPC program, which pays for five allied health consultations a year, chiropractic included.

Yes

WorkCover and the TAC both cover chiropractic. In either case you are covered for one of chiropractic, osteopathy or physiotherapy, not more than one.

Usually, under extras

Most private health insurers cover homoeopathy under their extras tables.

No

Medicare does not cover homoeopathy.

Dr John Cook

Dr John Cook is the principal of the centre, a chiropractor and homoeopath who has practised in Endeavour Hills for many years. For a long time he was also a part-time lecturer in Clinical Practice at the RMIT Department of Chiropractic, now retired.

His qualifications are a B.App.Sc. (Chiro.), a Grad.Dip. in Musculoskeletal Diagnosis, a Dip. Homoeopathy and the Diploma of the National Board of Chiropractic Examiners in the United States.

He is a member of the Australian Chiropractors Association and the Australian Homoeopathic Association, registered as a chiropractor, and listed on the Australian Register of Homoeopaths. As a chiropractor he looks at the whole picture: injuries, nutrition, lifestyle and stress, as well as the spine. Make a time to see Dr Cook.

Questions

Will adjustments make my spine too loose?

ChiropracticNo. Only the joints that need to be adjusted are adjusted.

Can I keep taking my usual medicines?

HomoeopathyYes. The two types of medicine work very differently and will not react with each other.

How many treatments will I need?

ChiropracticIt varies from person to person. It depends on how long the problem has been there, whether there is any pathology such as osteoarthritis, your lifestyle, your age and your vitality.

Should I stop my pharmaceutical medicines?

HomoeopathyNo. Only stop a pharmaceutical medicine under the direction of your medical doctor. If your symptoms improve, your doctor can reduce it.

Do I have to have treatment forever?

ChiropracticNo. Some people only want the problem they came in with looked after: they have a series of treatments and come back when they need to. Others prefer maintenance, and are seen anywhere from once a month to once every six months.

Do homoeopathic medicines have side effects?

HomoeopathyIt is very rare for people to react badly to homoeopathic medicines.
